---
title: Probabilistic Safety Is a Liability You Have Labeled as Protection
date: 2026-06-26
excerpt: You can lower the odds of a bad action. You cannot prove a particular decision was correct. In regulated industries, that difference is everything.
---

Most AI safety work reduces the probability of a bad output. That is genuinely useful, and for a consumer chatbot it is enough.

It is not enough for a regulated decision.

When an automated system denies a loan or rejects a claim, the institution does not get to say the model was probably fine. It has to defend that one decision, on its own, with evidence. A probability distribution is not evidence. It is a description of the model's behaviour in aggregate, and aggregates are exactly what a fair-lending examiner is trained to look past.

This is the quiet trap in a lot of current AI governance. Teams buy a tool that lowers the odds of harm and file it under compliance. But lowering odds and proving a specific outcome are different problems. The first is a model problem. The second is an enforcement and evidence problem, and it lives at the point where the decision actually executes.

If your defense to a regulator is statistical, you do not have a defense. You have a confidence interval.

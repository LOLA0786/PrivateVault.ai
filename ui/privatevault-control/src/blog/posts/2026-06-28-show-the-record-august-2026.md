---
title: "Show the Record: What Changes in August 2026"
date: 2026-06-28
excerpt: The EU AI Act's high-risk obligations turn explainability from best practice into law. Most institutions cannot yet reconstruct their own decisions.
---

For years the answer to "is your AI fair" has been some version of "trust us." That answer is running out of road.

In August 2026 the high-risk obligations of the EU AI Act take effect, including the record-keeping requirements in Article 12. Credit scoring and a range of financial decisions sit inside the high-risk category. In parallel, US fair-lending law already requires explainable adverse-action decisions, SR 11-7 governs model risk, and India's RBI is tightening accountability for digital lending.

The common thread is a shift in the burden of proof. The requirement is no longer be fair. It is show the record. Show why this decision was made, show that it stayed inside policy, and show that the record has not been altered since.

Here is the uncomfortable part. The moment an institution actually tries to produce that record for an AI-driven decision, many discover they cannot. The decision was made by a system that logged some of what happened, not all of it, and not in a form that survives scrutiny. The reconstruction is partial. The evidence does not hold.

That gap is not a future problem. The institutions running AI into regulated decisions today are already accumulating decisions they will not be able to fully defend. The work of fixing that has to start before the deadline, because you cannot retroactively generate evidence for a decision that already happened.
